## 0. The starting point that decides everything
- Median ~200 views/post, 1 hit at 1,690, ~0 followers, TikTok suppressed, YouTube effectively untested [R-old: LEARNINGS, STATE].
- At that base, **any per-view model earns almost nothing**. Example: the old plan's own rate (products + affiliate $0.10 per 1k views [R-old revenue-plan]) × 3 platforms × 30 posts × 200 views = **$1.80/month**. Even a 20× better buyer-intent rate gives $36/month.
- YPP before 2027-01-31 needs 10M Shorts views in 90 days ≈ 111k/day. From 200/post that is not a plan; treat YPP as a bonus, not a pillar.
- So the real question is: **what can be sold where the buyer does not need us to have views first?** Only one model below answers that (Model A). Models B and C need views (B) or a niche community (C).

Common formula used throughout:
`$/month = monthly views × link CTR × opt-in rate × (buy rate × average order + affiliate $/subscriber)` for audience models, and
`$/month = outreach × reply rate × call rate × close rate × average deal` for the service model.

## Model A · "Lowpoly Studio": low-poly 3D explainer Shorts sold to businesses (B2B service; channel = portfolio)

### Niche, audience, why they pay
- **Buyer:** small software/app businesses that need short product explainers for their listing, site and socials: **Shopify app developers** (Nathan already works in this world), SaaS/micro-SaaS founders, and **Nathan's own agency clients** (local businesses buying websites/AI receptionists; a "how it works" 30 s clip is an upsell to an existing relationship).
- **Why they pay:** a clear 30–45 s "how our product works" video is a known need (app-store listing video, landing-page hero, ad creative). Low-poly 3D looks premium and different from the stock-template/Canva explainers they otherwise buy, and we deliver in days because the kit exists. They pay for an outcome (a finished asset), not for our audience, so **0 followers is not a blocker.**
- **Channel's job:** a public portfolio and proof of craft, not revenue. Rename the channel to something neutral (e.g. "Lowpoly Explains"; trademark-check [V]) and post **mechanism Shorts** ("how X actually works"), which is exactly what a buyer wants to see from a vendor. Research angle C ("how/why is it built that way", fit 5/5 [R-old angle-research-yt]) is the content.

### 5 channel videos (portfolio + organic reach), proven format: number in line 1, twist, loop
All figures [V] at build time against a primary source.
1. **"A QR code can lose 30% of itself and still scan."** Twist: that's why brands put logos in the middle; the code repairs itself (Reed–Solomon, level H). Loop: "...so tear off 30% of it, and it still—" → "A QR code can lose 30%…".
2. **"A lift's counterweight weighs the car plus about 40% of its load."** Twist: the motor lifts almost nothing; it just tips a balance. Loop back to the counterweight dropping.
3. **"Every barcode is 95 stripes wide, and the last digit is a lie detector."** Twist: the check digit catches a misread scan. Loop: scanner beeps → first stripe.
4. **"Your card tap makes about 4 stops in under 2 seconds."** Twist: the shop doesn't actually get the money for days. Loop: the next tap starts the same journey. (Describe the flow only; no card brand logos [R-old LEARNINGS QA].)
5. **"A vending machine checks your coin 3 ways before it says yes."** Twist: a washer of the same size fails the magnetic test. Loop: coin drops back to the slot.
Each one doubles as a sales sample: "this is what your product could look like".

### Funnel
1. **Warm (week 2+):** Nathan mentions it to existing agency clients and Shopify-app contacts (he decides; no outreach by agents). 
2. **Cold (with Nathan's approval, later):** a lead list of Shopify apps whose App Store listing has **no video or an old one** (public, checkable signal; the repo has `lead-mining` and `cold-email` skills). Personalised email with a **free 10 s mock of their own product in low-poly** (we can make one in ~1 h on the kit). Sending is Nathan's call and out of scope for this round.
3. Portfolio page (one page: 5 samples, 3 packages, "book a 15-min call" / email). Channel bio links here.

### Arithmetic to $10k
Conversion assumptions [A] (cold B2B with a personalised free mock): reply 4%, of replies 40% take a call, of calls 35% buy. Warm referrals: assume 2 deals over the first 3 months.
`deals/month = emails × 0.04 × 0.40 × 0.35 = emails × 0.0056` → 200 emails/month ≈ **1.1 deals/month**.
Deal mix [A]: average first deal $700 (starter/launch mix); 1 in 4 clients converts to the monthly plan.

| Month | New deals | Revenue | Cumulative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nov 2026 | 0 (build portfolio + page) | $0 | $0 |
| Dec 2026 | 1 warm starter | $450 | $450 |
| Jan 2027 | 1 warm + 1 cold launch pack | $1,650 | $2,100 |
| Feb 2027 | 1–2 cold + 1 monthly starts | $2,300 | $4,400 |

**$1k/month: January 2027. $10k cumulative: April 2027.** Pessimistic (half the conversion, no monthly clients): ~$700/month from Feb → $10k ≈ **Q4 2027**. Concentration: $10k = about 8 starter-size deals + 2–3 retainers; no single whale needed.
Views needed: **none** for the money; the channel only needs to look credible.

### Time, cost, risks, kill criteria
- **Nathan:** 2–3 h/week (client calls, approvals, invoicing, deciding who to email). **Us:** ~6–16 agent-hours per deal plus 3–4 portfolio Shorts/week (~1 h each).
- **Cost:** existing Fish voice + tokens; maybe a $10–15/mo domain/page. Token cost per client piece is the main variable cost; price must cover it.
- **Risks:** (1) Nathan's sales time competes with his other businesses; (2) custom client scenes are slower than our 1 h kit videos (characters/rigs are the cost [R-old angle-research-yt B]); (3) AI video tools compress prices; our edge is 3D + speed + accuracy; (4) client revisions eat agent time: cap at 1 revision; (5) cold email rules/deliverability (Nathan's agency infra).
- **Kill criteria:** by **2027-01-15**, if ≥150 targeted emails (with free mocks) produced **<3 calls**, change the target (e.g. from Shopify apps to agencies white-labelling us). By **2027-02-28**, if **$0 paid**, kill the service model.

## Model B · "Side-Business Numbers": low-poly stories of how small businesses make money + a numbers product (B2C, audience-driven)

### Niche, audience, why they pay
- **Niche:** the real unit economics of businesses people can actually start or are curious about: vending machines, laundromats, car washes, food trucks, cinemas, coffee shops, printers-and-ink, Costco. Low-poly one-story format (the proven penny format) but every story is "where the money really comes from".
- **Audience:** would-be side-hustlers and small-business owners (aged ~20–40). This group **already buys**: business-plan templates, startup calculators and "start a vending route" courses are an established paid category [A: Researcher to size Etsy/Gumroad listings and prices].
- **Why they'd pay:** each video ends with a number they want to run for themselves ("what would one machine earn in my city?"). The product is the calculator behind the video, with sources. That is intent, unlike "fun money facts".
- Avoids the regulated zone (no credit, investing or crypto). Not "get rich" claims: show costs and failure points too, to stay factual and trustworthy.
- Brand: retire @lineitemfyi for a clear name, e.g. "How It Pays" [V trademark/handle].

### 5 videos (number line 1, twist, loop) — figures [V]
1. **"A cinema keeps about 85 cents of every popcorn dollar."** Twist: it barely profits on the ticket, because the studio takes about half. Loop: "…which is why popcorn costs—" → line 1.
2. **"Costco's hot dog has cost $1.50 since 1985."** Twist: Costco doesn't need it to profit; membership fees are about half of its operating income. Loop: next member walks to the hot-dog stand.
3. **"A $2 vending-machine soda costs the owner about 60 cents."** Twist: the biggest cost isn't the soda, it's the commission to the building. Loop: coin in again.
4. **"Your printer can cost less than 2 sets of its ink."** Twist: it's sold near cost on purpose; the ink is the business. Loop: empty cartridge → new printer box.
5. **"A car wash uses about 40 gallons per car, and recycles most of it."** Twist: water isn't its biggest bill; the land is. Loop: car rolls back in.

### Funnel and arithmetic
Assumptions [A]: bio-link CTR 0.4% of views (IG/YT Shorts; probably 0.1–1%), landing opt-in 35%, 4% of subscribers buy the $29 pack within 60 days, affiliate $0.40/subscriber.
`$ per 1k views = 1,000 × 0.004 × 0.35 × (0.04 × 29 + 0.40) = 1.4 subscribers × $1.56 ≈ $2.18`
- At today's 200 median × 90 posts/month (3 platforms) = 18k views/month → **$39/month**.
- **$1k/month needs ~460k views/month** ≈ 5k views per post across 3 platforms, i.e. **25× today's median.**
- $10k cumulative ≈ 4.6M views total.

Growth path [A] (the key uncertainty; winners' first 50k video came at upload #7–17 [R-old LEARNINGS 10-17]):
| Month | Views/month | $/month | Cumulative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nov–Dec 2026 | 40k → 120k | $90 → $260 | $350 |
| Jan 2027 | 250k | $545 | $900 |
| Feb 2027 | 460k | $1,000 | $1,900 |
| Mar–Jul 2027 | 600k avg | $1,300 | **$8,400** |
| Aug 2027 | 700k | $1,500 | **$9,900–10,500** |
**$1k/month: Feb 2027 (only if views grow 25×). $10k cumulative: ~Aug 2027.** If median stays ~200: **$10k takes >20 years.** This model is a bet on a second, third and fourth hit.

### Time, cost, risks, kill criteria
- **Nathan:** ~1 h/week (store/payout setup once, approvals). **Us:** 1 Short/day (~1 h each) + building 15 sheets once (~2 days of agent time) + a 5-email welcome sequence.
- **Cost:** ~$0–30/month (free email tier until ~1k subscribers [V]); store fees ~5–10% per sale [V].
- **Risks:** (1) needs views we've never had; (2) "how X makes money" is a cloned format in long-form (53% of new uploads <1k views [R-old LEARNINGS]), though less so as low-poly Shorts; (3) unit-economics figures are often estimates, hard to keep "facts only" with ≤4 figures; (4) IG makes links hard (one bio link, no links in Reels); (5) UAE store payout unconfirmed.
- **Kill criteria:** after **40 posts in the new niche (≈ 2026-12-15)**, if median views per post <2,000 **or** bio-link click-through <0.2%, drop the product path and keep only the content as portfolio. If **<20 email sign-ups by 2027-01-15**, don't build the paid pack.

## Model C · "Lowpoly Kit": sell our production system to creators and developers (B2C product to a niche community)

### Niche, audience, why they pay
- **Buyer:** devs and faceless creators who want to make code-rendered 3D Shorts (Remotion/Three.js users, Claude/AI-agent builders, faceless-channel hustlers). The Opus-era "code video" wave is a real interest spike in this crowd (703 showcase posts; median X views 600–1,000, breakouts to 1–3.8M [R-old angle-research-social]).
- **Why they pay:** building a working low-poly kit (story camera with a seamless loop, number plates, hook bar, pastel world, render pipeline, QA rubric) took us weeks; buying it saves them that. They pay for time saved, a known reason to buy dev templates.
- **Content:** "made with code" Shorts and build breakdowns, posted to X and YouTube (where devs are) plus Remotion/Three.js communities; the low-poly Shorts themselves are the demo.

### 5 videos (number line 1, twist, loop)
1. **"This 35-second video is about 600 lines of code."** [V count] Twist: no Blender, no camera, the camera is a function. Loop: the code scrolls back to line 1.
2. **"Our views went up 12× when we changed 1 thing."** Twist: not the topic; the format (140 → 1,690, penny [R-old]). Loop: the old video morphs into the new.
3. **"Every frame at the end of this video is the first frame."** Twist: the camera path is a closed curve, so the loop is invisible. Loop: literally.
4. **"1 prompt, 1 hour, 1 low-poly Short."** Twist: the slow part isn't rendering; it's fact-checking 4 numbers. Loop.
5. **"3 lines of code make this whole sea."** Twist: it's one plane with noise, not 10,000 polygons placed by hand. Loop.

## Recommendation: A as the engine, with the channel built to feed it (and B's content later)
1. **Build Model A now.** It is the only model where money does not wait for views we have never had, the payout rail already exists (Nathan's agency), and the buyer pays for something we can already make in hours. ~8 deals + 2–3 retainers = $10k by about April 2027.
2. **Re-aim the channel as the studio's portfolio:** mechanism Shorts ("how X works"), the proven low-poly story format, neutral new name. These are better sales samples than money trivia and travel as well (angle C evidence [R-old]).
3. **Keep B as an option, not a commitment:** if any portfolio Short passes ~10k views, add a free calculator/email capture to that topic and test demand before building a paid pack. YPP remains a bonus.
4. **Do C only as a by-product** after month 3, if A's delivery has hardened the kit; it competes with A for the same craft, so sell services first.

**What Nathan must do (A):** decide the studio name and whether it bills through his agency; confirm Stripe/bank invoicing; name 3–5 warm contacts (clients, Shopify-app peers) he is willing to show it to; 2–3 h/week for calls. **What we build in weeks 1–2:** 5 portfolio Shorts (above), 1 one-page studio site with 3 packages, 2 "free mock" samples on fictional products, a lead list of Shopify apps with no listing video (not contacted until Nathan approves).
