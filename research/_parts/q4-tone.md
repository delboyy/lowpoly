# Q4: Funny vs witty/dry vs plain professional for B2B-to-small-business short-form

Researched 2026-10-31. Scope: Reels / TikTok / Shorts aimed at business owners (agency, real estate, clinic, trades, e-commerce, Dubai/UAE).

## How to read the numbers
- **Views** for TikTok and YouTube were fetched directly with yt-dlp on 2026-10-31. Instagram views are **vidIQ-reported** (yt-dlp returns no view count for Reels); I could not verify them independently. They are marked (vidIQ).
- **Comments and likes** for all IG and TikTok items were fetched with yt-dlp on 2026-10-31 (public, no login).
- **Comment text was not retrievable.** Instagram blocks anonymous access, and yt-dlp returned zero comment bodies for TikTok. So "buyer intent" below is a **proxy only** (comment count per view, plus whether a comment-keyword CTA was in the caption). I did not see a single "how do I hire you" comment. Treat all intent claims as **unverified**.
- vidIQ credits ran out partway through (about 4 searches used), so the sample is what those searches surfaced, not a systematic sweep. No Shorts-only YouTube sample for tone could be built reliably.
- "Comm/view" = comments divided by views.

### Comment-rate summary (this sample only, not a study)
- Skits without a CTA: roughly 0.003% to 0.09% (one outlier at 0.19%).
- Plain pitch without a CTA: roughly 0% to 0.02%.
- Plain educational with an explicit comment CTA: roughly 0.09% to 0.40%.
- Caveat: a comment is not a buyer. Comment bait inflates counts. Also, @toxicofficiall (a skit-style ad for an Ajman real-estate office, 1.3M views, 5,127 comments, 0.39%, https://www.tiktok.com/@toxicofficiall/video/7684242928947973384) shows that a funny format with a "share and repost" ask can match the CTA numbers. Neither signal shows revenue.

## 2. Reach vs buyer intent: what I could and couldn't establish
- Reach: funny and dry formats win. Highest verified reach per account size came from skits (@bigwaveclean, @juliettelaundry, @mianoorrealestatellc).
- Buyer intent: **unverified.** No comment bodies were accessible. No post stated DMs or link clicks. The only stated downstream numbers are vendor claims (Vector +22% demo requests, +44% pipeline) and unread case-study headlines such as Social Savvy "776 leads from a 7-second reel" (https://www.socialsavvyhq.co/resources/case-study-how-we-turned-a-7-second-reel-into-776-leads-for-our-client, 2023, grade C/D, not read in full).
- Audience mismatch risk: many skit hits are made for peers (realtors joking about brokers, owners joking about spam calls). Peers like and share, but they are not necessarily buyers of a fix.

## 4. UAE / Gulf tone considerations
- **Formality is the default, but it varies.** Talaera (https://www.talaera.com/culture/gulf-business-culture-uae-qatar-saudi-compared/): formality "varies widely by emirate, sector, organization"; a Dubai playbook may not transfer to Doha or Riyadh. Grade C (consultancy blog).
- **Language:** U.S. Commercial Service says about 40% of the UAE is native Arabic-speaking, English is the business language, and Arabic is desirable and sometimes mandatory in consumer-facing advertising (https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/united-arab-emirates-selling-factors-and-techniques). Grade B (government guide). Note: it is about advertising generally, not Reels tone.
- **Bilingual mix:** HiDubai (https://focus.hidubai.com/what-makes-a-brand-trustworthy-to-uae-consumers-in-2026/) recommends an English primary feed with consistent Arabic touchpoints (a line in Arabic) rather than full translation. It also notes expats are fragmented (South Asian, Arab, Western, East African) "each with different references and humor," and that the most common risk is "a joke that didn't land." Also says imperfect, real-office video reads as more trustworthy, and tone for criticism should be calm and direct. Grade C (media blog).
- **Content rules:** same source notes UAE National Media Council guidelines (2026) require content to be respectful and transparent even when informal. A web-search summary also flagged avoiding sarcasm and disparaging remarks about Islamic culture, but I did not verify that against a primary page. Treat as unverified.
- **Local evidence of humor working:** Dubai realtors posted humor that reached well (@mianoorrealestatellc 2.1M, @anastasiia_gurskaya 380K, @karaplus_car_rental 857K+ on TikTok). Those jokes are about the UAE experience (visitor vs resident, fines, clients), not about belittling a business owner. Grade D (anecdote).
- Inference for the brand: jokes that point at a process ("the phone rings and nobody answers") should be safer than jokes that point at the owner ("you are losing money because you are lazy"). This is my inference, not a sourced finding.

## 5. Verdict
**Plain verdict: no tone wins outright, and the evidence is thin.**
- Funny wins reach. Plain professional with a clear comment CTA wins measurable comment actions in this small sample. Dry/witty sits between, with skit-like reach and the best UAE comment rate, but there is no verified lead data for it.
- Humor's research support is real but conditional (Swani 2025: helps attitude, weaker when unrelated to the product or when a relationship exists). No source shows humor producing paying SMB clients.
- Skits usually need faces and acting; Business Leaks is faceless, which favors voiceover plus numbers over performance comedy.

**Test first: dry/witty-lite, meaning a plain professional diagnosis with one deadpan line tied directly to the leak.** Example: state the lost-lead number in AED and add a single understated line. Reasons: (1) it keeps the humor related to the product, which is the one condition Swani found; (2) it matches UAE formality; (3) it is feasible faceless; (4) it still carries the LEAK CTA that drove the highest comment rates. Run three arms with the same leaks: plain professional (control), dry-lite, and one full skit-style arm as a reach experiment.
- Measure per 1,000 views: LEAK comments, DMs, free checks completed, purchases. Count qualified leads (UAE owners in target trades), not likes.
- Thin evidence flags: tiny sample from 4 vidIQ searches; IG views not independently verified; no comment text read; no intent data beyond a vendor claim; several accounts are peers' content, not sellers.
