Coke cost 5¢ for 73 years. Here's why.

From 1886 to 1959 a bottle of Coca-Cola cost a nickel. By 1950 the company owned most of America's vending machines, and they took a single coin. In 1953 Coca-Cola asked the US Treasury to mint a 7.5-cent coin; the Treasury said no. It then briefly planned to make one in every nine vending-machine bottles empty.

Sources: Levy & Young, "The Real Thing: Nominal Price Rigidity of the Nickel Coke, 1886–1959", Journal of Money, Credit and Banking (2004); NPR Planet Money, "Why Coke Cost A Nickel For 70 Years" (2012).
