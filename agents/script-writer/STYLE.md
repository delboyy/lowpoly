# Business Leaks: script and hook styles

Six voices for the same 30–40 s low-poly Short. Every sample script below uses the **same topic and the same Red-Team-approved facts as BL #2 (reply within an hour)**, so Nathan can compare tone, not content. The live, plain version of that video is `studio/samples/v3/reply-leaks.json`.

## Rules every style keeps

| Rule | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| 30–40 s spoken | ~85–110 words. The live scripts run ~2.8 words/s (reply: 89 words = 31.8 s). |
| Number in line 1 | The first spoken sentence carries the headline figure, and the hook bar shows it. Hook bar ≤ 8 words (RUBRIC B1). |
| ≤ 4 figures | Count every figure that appears on screen. Spoken glosses ("nearly one in five") are only used where the lead has approved them. |
| Seamless loop | The last line is an unfinished sentence that is completed by line 1 ("…Because right now," → "Seven in ten carts…"). Read the last line and the first line aloud together; it must sound like one sentence. |
| CTA | DM "LEAK" for a free check. It lives in the post description (and optionally an end card), never as a VO line that breaks the loop. |
| Facts | Use the lead's exact wording. Never round, never add a figure, never turn a dramatized moment into a claim ("everyone then calls a competitor" is a claim; "you put the phone down" is a scene). |
| Audience | **Worldwide** (Nathan, 2026-10-31). No city- or country-specific wording or figures (no Dubai, AED, UAE) unless the lead asks. Studies may be from one country, but signpost them ("in one US study"). |
| Humour guardrail | **The leak is the villain. The owner is the busy hero.** Jokes land on the chair, the spinner, the beep, the wait, never on the owner. Owners are busy, not lazy, stupid or greedy. |
| The fix | Phrase it as advice ("a reminder the day before"), not as a measured result ("cuts no-shows by half"), unless the lead has a source. |
| Fish TTS tags | Bracket tags at the start of a line, as in the live JSONs: `[calm, curious]`, `[lowered]`, `[quiet]`. New styles add `[flat]`, `[hushed]`, `[smug]`, `[delighted]`, `[nervous]`, `[punchy]`. |

### Red Team lessons (BL #4–#6, plan/BL-SCRIPTS-2-factcheck.md)
- **Check that the fix isn't contradicted by the same source.** The VA no-show study found reminders barely helped, so the fix became "a one-tap way to cancel or rebook". Read the source's own findings before writing the fix line.
- **Name the unit the study actually measured.** The VA data is "one big US veterans' hospital" (Houston, twelve years of records), not a "health system". Costs fall on "the hospital".
- **Observational studies don't cause anything.** Write "when sites got faster, conversion rates rose", not "make it faster and conversions go up".
- **Use the study's exact sample wording.** "37 big brands, tracked for thirty days", not "four weeks".
- **Don't add details the source doesn't support.** "Booked, confirmed…" was cut because "confirmed" isn't in the data.
- **Hedge sympathetic scenes.** "*Maybe* they were under someone else's sink", not "they were".
- **Hashtags count too.** #dentist was cut because the study is a hospital, not dental.
- **Chat/call mock-ups:** label "Illustrative chat/call" from frame 1 (over the hook bar too). Use a generic messenger UI (no WhatsApp/portal logos or trade dress). Keep the competitor's reply before the time-skip, or tag it "Meanwhile". No money counters without a source: count missed calls instead. Use "Callers left waiting: 0", not "customers lost". The caller hangs up before the tone.
- **Auto-replies aren't the whole fix.** WAV excluded auto-replies, so the fix is "an instant first reply, then a person who follows up fast".
- **Jokes can't imply evidence.** "Replay shows two fixes" was cut because Baymard measures reasons, not fixes.
- Glosses that passed: "nearly one in five" (18.8%; for Baymard's 18%, the base must be "shoppers ready to buy"), "more than one in four" (27%).

Visual notes in the samples are suggestions for the Creative Editor, not instructions.

## 1. Deadpan narrator ("The Dry One")

**What it is:** a calm, flat voice that states awful numbers as if reading a weather report. The joke is the gap between the tone and the loss. Short sentences, one dry aside per beat.
**Best for:** any topic. Lowest risk; closest to what's live, just funnier.
**Watch out for:** too many asides kill pace. One per beat, max.

**Hooks**
1. Bar: `7 in 10 online carts never check out` / VO: "Seven in ten online carts are abandoned before checkout. Nobody panics. Except the cart."
2. Bar: `18.8% average no-show rate` / VO: "In one US health system, the average no-show rate was eighteen point eight percent. The chair took it well."
3. Bar: `27% of home-services calls: unanswered` / VO: "Twenty-seven percent of calls to home-services businesses go unanswered. The phone is fine. Thanks for asking."

**Sample script (reply topic)**
| # | VO | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [flat] Reply to a new lead within an hour, and you're nearly seven times more likely to get a real conversation with the buyer than if you wait even an hour longer. | **~7×** likelier to qualify the lead |
| 2 | That's from more than a million leads, at forty-two US companies. Not a hunch. | |
| 3 | So researchers tested over two thousand companies. | |
| 4 | Just over a third replied within the hour. | **37%** replied within the hour |
| 5 | [lowered] Almost one in four never replied at all. | **23%** never replied |
| 6 | [flat] Never. The lead is, presumably, still waiting. Holding a coffee. Going cold. | |
| 7 | [flat] Anyway. If a message has been sitting in your inbox since lunch, just know: | → loops to 1 |

## 2. POV: you're the lost customer

**What it is:** second person. The viewer *is* the customer (or the cart, or the lead) living through the leak in real time. Builds empathy, so the owner watching feels the customer's side without being blamed.
**Best for:** missed calls, slow sites, unanswered inquiries: anything with a waiting moment.
**Watch out for:** the POV is one person's scene. Don't say what "customers do next" unless it's sourced.

**Hooks**
1. Bar: `POV: you're a shopping cart` / VO: "POV: you're a shopping cart. Seven in ten of you never make it to checkout."
2. Bar: `POV: you asked about a home` / VO: "POV: you asked about a home online. In one US study, forty-eight percent of inquiries like yours never got a reply from an agent."
3. Bar: `POV: your pipe burst. Their phone rings.` / VO: "POV: water's coming through your ceiling, and twenty-seven percent of calls to home-services businesses go unanswered."

**Sample script (reply topic)**
| # | VO | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [calm, curious] POV: you're a new lead. Get a reply within the hour, and that business is nearly seven times likelier to actually talk with you than if it waits even an hour longer. | **~7×** likelier to qualify the lead |
| 2 | You're one of more than a million leads, at forty-two US companies. | |
| 3 | So you hit send. And refresh. And refresh. | |
| 4 | In a test of over two thousand companies, just over a third answered within the hour. | **37%** replied within the hour |
| 5 | [lowered] Almost one in four never answered at all. | **23%** never replied |
| 6 | [quiet] Your coffee goes cold. So does your interest. | |
| 7 | [quiet] Okay. Let's rewind. | → loops to 1 |

## 3. Mock documentary ("Nature doc")

**What it is:** a hushed, reverent wildlife-documentary narrator observing the leak "in its natural habitat". Low-poly 3D suits it perfectly (slow camera, a lone creature, a sunrise).
**Best for:** leaks with a visible "creature": the empty chair, the abandoned cart, the unanswered message.
**Watch out for:** the whisper can feel slow. Keep lines short so it still lands in 30–40 s.

**Hooks**
1. Bar: `Observe: the abandoned cart, in the wild` / VO: "Seven in ten online carts are abandoned before checkout. Here, we observe one."
2. Bar: `18.8%: the no-show, observed` / VO: "In one US health system, the average no-show rate was eighteen point eight percent. Let us observe one in the wild."
3. Bar: `The rare lead answered within the hour` / VO: "The lead answered within the hour. A rare creature. Nearly seven times likelier to become a real conversation."

**Sample script (reply topic)**
| # | VO | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [hushed] The lead answered within the hour. A rare creature. Nearly seven times likelier to become a real conversation than one left waiting even an hour longer. | **~7×** likelier to qualify the lead |
| 2 | Researchers tracked more than a million of them, across forty-two US companies. | |
| 3 | [hushed] But the species is under threat. | |
| 4 | Of over two thousand companies tested, just over a third replied within the hour. | **37%** replied within the hour |
| 5 | [lowered] And almost one in four never replied at all. | **23%** never replied |
| 6 | [hushed] The lead waits. The lead grows cold. Nature can be cruel. | |
| 7 | [hushed] But if you move quickly, you may yet glimpse it: | → loops to 1 |

## 4. Countdown / listicle

**What it is:** "3 numbers…", counted down, with the biggest number last. Punchy, high retention ("wait for number one"). The list items are the sourced figures themselves, so nothing extra is invented.
**Best for:** topics with 2–3 strong figures (reply, carts). Not for single-stat topics (#6 trades).
**Watch out for:** the 4-figure cap. Three items is the sweet spot.

**Hooks**
1. Bar: `3 numbers your inbox won't like` / VO: "Three numbers your inbox won't like. Number three: just over a third."
2. Bar: `2 numbers behind every empty chair` / VO: "Two numbers behind every empty chair. Number two: eighteen point eight percent."
3. Bar: `The #1 reason carts die at checkout` / VO: "Seven in ten online carts are abandoned. Counting down to the number one reason."

**Sample script (reply topic)**
| # | VO | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [punchy] Three numbers your inbox won't like. Number three: just over a third. | |
| 2 | In a test of over two thousand US companies, just over a third replied to a new lead within the hour. | **37%** replied within the hour |
| 3 | Number two: almost one in four. | |
| 4 | [lowered] That's how many never replied at all. | **23%** never replied |
| 5 | [punchy] And number one: nearly seven times. | |
| 6 | Reply within the hour, and you're nearly seven times likelier to get a real conversation with the buyer than if you wait even an hour longer. That's across more than a million leads. | **~7×** likelier to qualify the lead |
| 7 | [quiet] So if one's sitting in your inbox right now, here are the | → loops to 1 |

## 5. Villain monologue (the leak speaks)

**What it is:** the leak itself narrates, smug and theatrical: the Empty Chair, the Loading Spinner, the Voicemail Beep, the Wait. It loves the bad numbers and is nervous about the fix. This makes "the leak is the villain" literal, and the fix becomes the hero by being the villain's fear.
**Best for:** every topic; strongest character and most "series-able" (a recurring cast of leaks).
**Watch out for:** the villain must never sneer at owners. Its line about them is "they're busy, that's all I need."

**Hooks**
1. Bar: `I'm the empty chair. 18.8%.` / VO: "In one US health system, the average no-show rate was eighteen point eight percent. Hi. I'm the empty chair."
2. Bar: `The loading spinner fears 0.1 seconds` / VO: "Make a mobile site a tenth of a second faster, and retail conversions went up eight point four percent. I'm the loading spinner. I hate that number."
3. Bar: `I'm the message nobody answered` / VO: "In one test, almost one in four companies never replied to a new lead. I'm the message they never answered."

**Sample script (reply topic). Villain: "the Wait"**
| # | VO | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [smug, theatrical] Reply within an hour, and you're nearly seven times likelier to get a real conversation with the buyer than if you wait even an hour longer. Which is why I, the Wait, adore an hour and one minute. | **~7×** likelier to qualify the lead |
| 2 | That's from more than a million leads, at forty-two US companies. | |
| 3 | When researchers tested over two thousand companies, just over a third beat me. | **37%** replied within the hour |
| 4 | [delighted] And almost one in four never replied at all. | **23%** never replied |
| 5 | Oh, they're not lazy. They're busy. Busy is all I need. | |
| 6 | [nervous] What do I hate? An instant reply that goes out while you're busy. | |
| 7 | [smug] But go on, finish your coffee. Just remember: | → loops to 1 |

## 6. Myth vs reality

**What it is:** a common belief, then the number that breaks it. Two or three myth/reality pairs. Feels like insider knowledge; very shareable.
**Best for:** topics where owners hold a comfortable assumption (speed doesn't matter, leads will wait).
**Watch out for:** every "myth" must be refuted by a sourced number in the same script. No straw men about owners ("Myth: owners don't care").

**Hooks**
1. Bar: `Myth: a tenth of a second is nothing` / VO: "Myth: a tenth of a second doesn't matter. Reality: making a mobile site that much faster lifted retail conversions eight point four percent."
2. Bar: `Myth: nobody minds making an account` / VO: "Myth: shoppers don't mind making an account. Reality: nearly one in five left because the site made them."
3. Bar: `Myth: every home inquiry gets a reply` / VO: "Myth: every home inquiry gets a reply. Reality: in one US study, forty-eight percent never got a reply from an agent."

**Sample script (reply topic)**
| # | VO | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [confident] Myth: if a lead's interested, a few hours won't hurt. Reality: reply within the hour and you're nearly seven times likelier to get a real conversation than if you wait even an hour longer. | **~7×** likelier to qualify the lead |
| 2 | That's from more than a million leads, at forty-two US companies. | |
| 3 | Myth: everyone replies fast these days. | |
| 4 | Reality: of over two thousand companies tested, just over a third replied within the hour. | **37%** replied within the hour |
| 5 | Myth: no real business just never replies. | |
| 6 | [lowered] Reality: almost one in four never replied at all. | **23%** never replied |
| 7 | [quiet] So next time someone says leads are patient, remember the first | → loops to 1 |

## Quick comparison

| Style | Funny level | Trust risk | Retention trick | Best topics |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deadpan | ●●○ | Low | Dry asides | All |
| POV | ●○○ | Low | Viewer lives it | Calls, speed, inquiries |
| Mock doc | ●●● | Low | Character + slow reveal | No-shows, carts |
| Countdown | ●○○ | Low | "Wait for #1" | Multi-figure topics |
| Villain | ●●● | Medium (watch the owner line) | Recurring character | All; best as a series |
| Myth vs reality | ●●○ | Medium (myths must be refuted by data) | "Insider knowledge" | Speed, replies |
